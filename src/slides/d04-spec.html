In expectation the output has the same distribution as the large model alone. vLLM V1 proposes with n-gram, EAGLE or Medusa.

Source: A. Gordić, Inside vLLM (2025); Chen et al. (2023). Probabilities are illustrative.

Problem: every token needs a full pass of a large model, reading all its weights. Three tokens cost three passes.

Idea: let something cheap guess several tokens ahead. Here it proposes three: the, cat, ran.

Then the large model runs once over the context and all the guessed tokens together, and now we have its probability at each position. Go left to right. If the large model likes a token at least as much as the draft did, accept it. If not, accept it with probability equal to the ratio. Here "the" is accepted. "cat" is a coin flip at 0.6, and happens to pass. "ran" has only a 0.2 chance, and is rejected, so we stop there.

At the rejected position we sample a replacement from a corrected distribution: large minus draft, floored at zero, then normalized. If all were accepted, we get one more for free. So one large pass gave three tokens. The accept rule is chosen so the result has exactly the distribution of the large model on its own. This is one of those results worth reading the proof of. vLLM V1 does not use a small LLM as the draft; it uses cheaper methods: n-gram lookup in the existing text, EAGLE and Medusa.
