The constructor builds the outer engine. The config holds every setting. The processor validates and tokenizes the input. The output processor turns raw results into what your program sees. In the middle is a client that talks to the engine core. In this example the core is in the same process; later it will be elsewhere, and this client is why nothing else has to change. That is a good design lesson by itself.

The core has three parts. The model executor drives forward passes on a worker. The structured output manager is for later. The scheduler decides which requests go into the next pass. It has a policy, a waiting queue and a running queue.

And it has the KV cache manager, the heart of paged attention. It keeps a free_block_queue: a pool of free blocks, often hundreds of thousands of them. If you remember the free list from your allocator lectures, this is one.
